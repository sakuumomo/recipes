# Malatang (麻辣烫)

Serves 2. About 55 minutes.

Noodles in a bowl. Soak water in a heavy saucepan. Broth and soup on the Mosaic at 300°F. The Mosaic is at 1500 watts.

Cut the pot and mix the bowls while the soak water cools. Pour it over the noodles, then start the broth. Cook the soup when a strand bends.

Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.

Lines set close together share a cup.

## Noodles

Use dried sweet potato cords about as thick as a chopstick. Thin mung bean threads fall apart in this soup.

- 2 oz sweet potato noodles (红薯粉条)

<!-- cup -->

- 3 cups water, for the noodles

1. Put the **sweet potato noodles** in a bowl.
2. Put the **water for the noodles** in a heavy saucepan. Set the saucepan fully on a coil turned to high. Stay with it. When the water steams, about 5 minutes, pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off. Go on to the pot and the bowls while it cools.
3. Pour the **water for the noodles** over them. Leave them until a strand bends without snapping, about 30 minutes. Go on to the broth. If a strand still snaps when the broth is ready, wait. Drain them.

## Pot

Use firm tofu, the block marked 北豆腐, small bok choy, and the thin marbled slices sold for hot pot. Do this before the wok is hot.

- 8 oz firm tofu (北豆腐)

<!-- cup -->

- 1 bunch enoki mushrooms (金针菇)

<!-- cup -->

- 6 oz hot-pot beef slices (肥牛)

<!-- cup -->

- 1 handful bok choy (小白菜), cut into 2 in pieces

1. Cut the **firm tofu** into 3/4 in cubes with the 8 in cleaver and pat the cubes dry. Trim the base off the **enoki mushrooms**, tear them into small bunches, and rinse them. If the **hot-pot beef slices** are frozen together, separate them while they are still cold. Pat them dry. Cut the **bok choy** into 2 in pieces and rinse it. Put each group into one cup or pile.

## Bowls

Use toasted Chinese sesame paste, and plain red chili oil.

- 2 tbsp sesame paste (芝麻酱)
- 2 tbsp hot water, for the paste

<!-- cup -->

- 1 clove garlic, minced
- 2 tsp Zhenjiang vinegar (镇江香醋)
- 1/4 tsp sugar, for the bowls
- 1 tsp chili oil (红油), for the bowls
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (香油)

<!-- cup -->

- white of 1 scallion, halved lengthwise and chopped
- green of 1 scallion, chopped
- 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped

1. Mince the **garlic** with the 8 in cleaver. Halve the **white of 1 scallion** lengthwise and chop it. Chop the **green of 1 scallion**. They come from the same scallion. Chop the **cilantro**. Set them aside.
2. Stir the **sesame paste** and the **hot water for the paste** together until the paste is smooth and falls slowly from the spoon.
3. Add the **garlic**, the **Zhenjiang vinegar**, the **sugar for the bowls**, the **chili oil for the bowls**, and the **toasted sesame oil** together. Stir until the sauce looks even. Spoon it into two bowls, half in each.
4. If a hand cannot stay on the side of the saucepan, wait until it can. Go back and finish the noodles.

## Broth

Use whole dried erjingtiao marked 二荆条, the coarse Pixian paste, and medium 二荆条 flakes marked 中辣.

- 2 tbsp peanut oil

<!-- cup -->

- 3 dried erjingtiao (干二荆条), snipped in half, loose seeds shaken out
- 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns (红花椒)

<!-- cup -->

- 1 in ginger, sliced
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tsp Sichuan chili flakes (四川辣椒面)
- 1 tbsp chili oil (红油), for the broth

<!-- cup -->

- 4 cups water, for the broth
- 2 tsp light soy sauce (生抽)
- 1 tsp sugar, for the broth
- 1 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal

<!-- cup -->

- 3/4 cup whole milk

1. Snip the **dried erjingtiao** in half and shake out the loose seeds. Slice the **ginger** and the **garlic** with the 8 in cleaver. Chop the **Pixian doubanjiang**. Put each group into one cup.
2. Seat the wok. Press start. Turn the left dial to 300°F. Turn the right dial to 40 minutes. Add the **peanut oil**. When it shimmers, add the **dried erjingtiao** and the **Sichuan peppercorns** together. Keep them in the bottom. Stir until the peppercorns smell citrusy and the chilies turn one shade darker, still red, about 20 seconds. If they blacken, press pause/clear once and stir until they stop darkening. Press start.
3. Add the **ginger** and the **garlic** together. Stir until they smell sweet, about 20 seconds. If they color, press pause/clear once and stir until they cool. Press start.
4. Add the **Pixian doubanjiang**. Keep it in the bottom. Stir until the oil turns red and the paste smells sweet, about 45 seconds. If it smells sharp, press pause/clear once and stir until the smell sweetens. Press start. If the oil has not turned red, turn the left dial up 10°F and keep frying until it is red.
5. Add the **Sichuan chili flakes** and the **chili oil for the broth**. Stir until the oil is red, about 15 seconds. If the oil smokes, press pause/clear once and stir until the smoke stops. Press start.
6. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Add the **water for the broth**, the **light soy sauce**, the **sugar for the broth**, and the **kosher salt** together. Stay with it. When the broth boils, turn the left dial to 250°F. The surface should bubble gently. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F.
7. Simmer until the broth smells numbing, about 8 minutes. The surface should bubble gently. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F.
8. Lift out the **dried erjingtiao**, the **Sichuan peppercorns**, the **ginger**, and the **garlic** with the wok ladle until the broth is clear of them.
9. Add the **whole milk**. Stir until the broth turns opaque and the surface shivers, about 1 minute. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. Taste. If it is flat, stir in one more pinch of the kosher salt. If you want it, stir in one pinch of MSG (味精).
10. If a strand of the sweet potato noodles still snaps, press on/off and wait until a strand bends. Seat the wok. Press start. Turn the left dial to 250°F. The surface should shiver. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F.

## Soup

These amounts were measured above.

**Noodles**

- 2 oz sweet potato noodles (红薯粉条), drained

**Pot**

- 8 oz firm tofu (北豆腐)

<!-- cup -->

- 1 bunch enoki mushrooms (金针菇)

<!-- cup -->

- 6 oz hot-pot beef slices (肥牛)

<!-- cup -->

- 1 handful bok choy (小白菜), cut into 2 in pieces

**Bowls**

- white of 1 scallion, halved lengthwise and chopped
- green of 1 scallion, chopped
- 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped

1. Drain the sweet potato noodles. Add the **drained sweet potato noodles** and press them under the surface. Cook until they turn translucent at the edges, about 6 minutes. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F.
2. Lay the **firm tofu** on the noodles. Cook until the noodles are chewy and the tofu is hot, about 2 minutes. Break one strand. The center is translucent. If the center is still white, cook until it turns translucent, about 5 minutes more.
3. Add the **enoki mushrooms**, the **hot-pot beef slices**, and the **bok choy** together. Stir once. Cook until the pink is gone and the slices are still tender, the mushrooms have wilted, and the bok choy leaves have wilted with the stems still crisp, about 45 seconds. If the beef stiffens, stop there. Press pause/clear once.
4. Ladle the soup into the two bowls with the wok ladle. Scatter the **white of 1 scallion**, the **green of 1 scallion**, and the **cilantro** over the top. The herbs should sit on the soup.
5. Press on/off. Set the wok on its stand.
