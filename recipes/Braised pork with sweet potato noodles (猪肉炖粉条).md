# Braised pork with sweet potato noodles (猪肉炖粉条)

Serves 2. About 90 minutes.

The noodle water heats in a heavy saucepan. The pork blanches in that saucepan. The stew starts on the Mosaic at 375°F. The Mosaic is at 1500 watts.

Soak the noodles first. Blanch the pork while they soften. If a strand still snaps when the pork is ready, wait. Then stew until a chunk yields to a fork, boil the gravy down, and add the noodles.

Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.

## Noodles

Use dried sweet potato cords about as thick as a chopstick. Thin mung bean threads fall apart in this stew.

- 3 oz sweet potato noodles (红薯粉条)

<!-- cup -->

- 4 cups water, for the noodles

1. Put the **sweet potato noodles** in a bowl.
2. Put the **water for the noodles** in a heavy saucepan. Set the saucepan fully on a coil turned to high. Stay with it. When the water steams, about 5 minutes, pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off. Leave it until you can keep a hand on the side of the saucepan.
3. Pour the **water for the noodles** over them. Leave them until a strand bends without snapping, about 30 minutes. Go on to the pork. If a strand still snaps when the pork is ready, wait. Drain them.

## Pork

Leave the skin on.

- 12 oz pork belly

<!-- cup -->

- 6 cups water, for the blanch

1. Cut the **pork belly** into 1 in chunks with the cleaver.
2. Put the **pork belly** and the **water for the blanch** in the heavy saucepan together. Set the saucepan fully on a coil turned to high. Stay with it. Skim the gray foam as it rises. When the water boils, cook 2 minutes. Pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off.
3. Lift the pork out. Rinse it under hot water until the scum is gone, and drain it. Pour off the blanch water.

## Stew

Do this before the wok is hot. Use salted Shaoxing.

- 1 tbsp peanut oil

<!-- cup -->

- 12 oz pork belly, blanched

<!-- cup -->

- 1 in ginger, sliced
- 2 scallions, cut into 2 in lengths
- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 1 star anise (八角)
- 1 piece cassia bark (桂皮), 2 in

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴料酒)
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce (生抽)
- 1 tsp dark soy sauce (老抽)
- 1 tsp sugar

<!-- cup -->

- 2 cups water, for the stew

<!-- cup -->

- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal
- 3 oz sweet potato noodles (红薯粉条), drained

1. Slice the **ginger** and cut the **scallions** into 2 in lengths with the cleaver. Smash the **garlic** with the side of the cleaver until the cloves split. Put each group into one cup or pile, prepared the way the line says.
2. Seat the wok. Press start. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Turn the right dial to 90 minutes. Add the **peanut oil**. When it shimmers, add the **pork belly, blanched**, and keep it in the bottom. Stir until the water boils off and the edges turn golden, about 6 minutes. If the oil smokes, press pause/clear once and stir until the smoke stops. Press start.
3. Turn the left dial to 300°F. Add the **ginger**, the **scallions**, the **garlic**, the **star anise**, and the **cassia bark** together. Stir until they smell sweet, about 1 minute. If they smell sharp, press pause/clear once and stir until the smell sweetens. Press start.
4. Add the **Shaoxing wine**, the **light soy sauce**, the **dark soy sauce**, and the **sugar** together. Stir until the pork looks reddish-brown, about 30 seconds.
5. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Pour in the **water for the stew**. Stay until it boils. Skim any foam.
6. Press watts until 600 shows. Turn the left dial to 185°F. Cover the wok with the glass lid. When the center boils, press pause/clear once. Press start when the surface has fallen back to a small bubble. If the surface stays still and never boils, turn the left dial up 10°F. Simmer until a fork pushed into a chunk meets little resistance, about 45 minutes. If the display reads 0, press start, turn the left dial to 185°F, and set the remaining time on the right dial. If 600 is not showing, press watts until it does. If the display reads E7, press pause/clear twice and wait until the fan stops. Seat the wok, press start, and turn the left dial to 185°F. If 600 is not showing, press watts until it does.
7. If 1500 is not showing, press watts until it does. Uncover the wok. Lift out the star anise and the cassia bark. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Cook until the sauce coats a spoon and the pork still sits in a loose gravy, about 8 minutes. If it still runs like water, keep cooking until it coats a spoon. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles. Press start. If the bottom starts to catch, press pause/clear once and stir until it releases. Press start.
8. Turn the left dial to 300°F. Add the **kosher salt** and the **sweet potato noodles, drained** together. Press the noodles under the surface. Cook until a strand is chewy and the center of a strand is translucent, about 6 minutes. If the center of a strand is still white, cook until it turns translucent, about 3 minutes more. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles. Press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F. If the bottom starts to catch, press pause/clear once and stir until it releases. Press start.
9. If 1500 is not showing, press watts until it does. Press on/off. Taste. If it is flat, stir in one more pinch of the kosher salt. If you want it, stir in one pinch of MSG (味精). Spoon it into a bowl. Eat it hot. The noodles keep drinking the gravy as it sits. Set the wok on its stand.

---

Add 2 tsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped, after the ginger, the scallions, the garlic, the star anise, and the cassia bark smell sweet. Stir until the oil turns red, about 30 seconds. If it smells sharp, press pause/clear once and stir until the smell sweetens. Press start. If the oil has not turned red, turn the left dial up 10°F and keep frying until it is red. Then go on to the Shaoxing wine.

Use sour napa cabbage from the bag marked 酸菜. Rinse and squeeze a handful, cut it into 1 in pieces, and add it with the water for the stew. Skip the kosher salt. The cabbage is already salty.
