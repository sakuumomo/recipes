# Braised pork with sweet potato noodles (猪肉炖粉条)

Serves 2. About 90 minutes.

The noodles soak in a bowl. The pork blanches, then stews, in a heavy saucepan.

Soak the noodles first. Blanch the pork while they soften. Stew until a chunk yields to a fork, then add the noodles.

Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.

## Ingredients

**Noodles**

- 3 oz sweet potato noodles (红薯粉条)
- 4 cups water, for the noodles

**Pork**

- 12 oz pork belly
- 6 cups water, for the blanch

**Stew**

- 1 tsp peanut oil
- 1 tbsp ginger, sliced
- 2 scallions, cut into 2 in lengths
- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 1 star anise (八角)
- 1 piece cassia bark (桂皮), 2 in
- 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴酒)
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce (生抽)
- 1 tsp dark soy sauce (老抽)
- 1 tsp sugar
- 3 1/2 cups water, for the stew
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal

---

## Noodles

Use dried sweet potato cords about as thick as a chopstick. Thin mung bean threads fall apart in this stew.

- 3 oz sweet potato noodles (红薯粉条)
- 4 cups water, for the noodles

1. Put the **sweet potato noodles** in a bowl.
2. Put the **water for the noodles** in a heavy saucepan on high until it steams, about 5 minutes. Pull the saucepan off the coil. Wait until a finger can stay in the water.
3. Pour the **water for the noodles** over them. Leave them until a strand bends without snapping, about 30 minutes. Go on to the pork. If a strand still snaps when the pork is ready, wait.

## Pork

Leave the skin on.

- 12 oz pork belly
- 6 cups water, for the blanch

1. Cut the **pork belly** into 1 in chunks.
2. Put the **pork belly** and the **water for the blanch** in the heavy saucepan. Set it on high. Skim the gray foam as it rises. When the water boils, cook 2 minutes.
3. Lift the pork out. Rinse it under hot water until the scum is gone, and drain it. Pour off the blanch water and wipe the saucepan dry.

## Stew

Use unsalted Shaoxing.

- 1 tsp peanut oil
- 12 oz pork belly, blanched
- 1 tbsp ginger, sliced
- 2 scallions, cut into 2 in lengths
- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 1 star anise (八角)
- 1 piece cassia bark (桂皮), 2 in
- 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴酒)
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce (生抽)
- 1 tsp dark soy sauce (老抽)
- 1 tsp sugar
- 3 1/2 cups water, for the stew
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal
- 3 oz sweet potato noodles (红薯粉条), drained

1. Put the saucepan on medium. Add the **peanut oil**. When it shimmers, about 1 minute, add the **blanched pork belly**. Stir until the water boils off and the edges turn golden, about 6 minutes. If the oil smokes, pull the saucepan off the coil.
2. Add the **ginger**, **scallions**, **garlic**, **star anise**, and **cassia bark** together. Stir until they smell sweet, about 1 minute.
3. Add the **Shaoxing wine**, **light soy sauce**, **dark soy sauce**, and **sugar** together. Stir until the pork looks reddish-brown, about 30 seconds.
4. Pour in the **water for the stew**. Turn the coil to high. When it boils, skim any foam. Pull the saucepan fully off the coil. Turn the coil to low and wait until it stops glowing. Set the saucepan fully back on. The surface should bubble gently. Cover it.
5. Simmer until a fork pushed into a chunk meets little resistance, about 45 minutes. If the center boils hard, pull the saucepan fully off until the surface settles, then set it fully back on. If it boils hard again, leave it off until the surface needs heat. If the surface goes still, turn the coil one setting higher and leave the saucepan fully on.
6. Lift out the star anise and the cassia bark.
7. Turn the coil to medium and leave the saucepan fully on. Add the **kosher salt** and the **drained sweet potato noodles** together. Press the noodles under the surface. Cook, uncovered, until they are chewy and the gravy still pools in a spoon, about 10 minutes. Break one strand. The center is translucent. If the center is still white, cook until it turns translucent, about 5 minutes more. If the center boils hard, pull the saucepan fully off until the surface settles, then set it fully back on. If the bottom starts to catch, pull the saucepan fully off and stir until it releases, then set it fully back on.
8. Eat it hot. The noodles keep drinking the gravy as it sits.
