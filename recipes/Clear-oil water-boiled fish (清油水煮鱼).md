# Clear-oil water-boiled fish (清油水煮鱼)

Serves 2. About 45 minutes.

The broth on the Mosaic at 375°F. The pour oil in a small skillet. The Mosaic is at 1500 watts.

Marinate the fish. While it sits, cut the chilies and the aromatics. When the fish timer rings, finish the step you are in, then keep going. When the broth reaches a shiver, cook the sprouts, then start the pour oil. Poach the fish when that oil is ready.

## Fish

Use salted Shaoxing.

- 8.8 oz snakehead fillet (黑鱼), skinless and boneless, sliced 1/8 in thick
- 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴料酒), for the fish
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal, for the fish
- 1/4 tsp ground white pepper, for the fish

<!-- cup -->

- 1 egg white

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tbsp sweet potato starch (红薯淀粉)

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tsp peanut oil, for the fish

1. Use the boning knife. If the **snakehead fillet** still has skin, cut the skin off. Slice the fillet on a bias, 1/8 in thick.
2. Rinse the slices under cold water until the water runs clear. Pat them dry. Put them in a bowl.
3. Add the **Shaoxing wine for the fish**, the **kosher salt for the fish**, and the **ground white pepper for the fish** together. Stir until the slices feel sticky.
4. Add the **egg white** and stir until every slice looks slick.
5. Add the **sweet potato starch** and stir until each slice has a thin coat.
6. Drizzle the **peanut oil for the fish** over the top and turn the slices once. Set a timer for 15 minutes. Go on to the prep. When the timer rings, finish the step you are in. The fish can wait longer if the broth is not ready yet.

## Prep

Do this while the fish marinates. Use whole dried erjingtiao marked 二荆条.

- 1 in ginger, sliced
- 3 scallion whites, cut into 2 in lengths

<!-- cup -->

- 2 handfuls soybean sprouts (黄豆芽)

<!-- cup -->

- 3 scallion greens, cut into 2 in lengths
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced thin
- 10 dried erjingtiao (干二荆条), snipped in half, loose seeds shaken out
- 1 tbsp green Sichuan peppercorns (青花椒)

1. Slice the **ginger**. Separate the whites and the greens of 3 scallions. Cut the **scallion whites** and the **scallion greens** into 2 in lengths. Keep them in two piles.
2. Rinse the **soybean sprouts** until they look clean.
3. Slice the **garlic** thin.
4. Snip the **dried erjingtiao** in half and shake out the loose seeds.
5. Put the **ginger** and the **scallion whites** in one cup. Leave the **soybean sprouts** in their own pile. Put the **scallion greens**, the **garlic**, the **dried erjingtiao**, and the **green Sichuan peppercorns** in another cup. Set the cups next to a large heatproof bowl.

## Broth

- 1 in ginger, sliced
- 3 scallion whites, cut into 2 in lengths

<!-- cup -->

- 3 cups unsalted chicken stock
- 1 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal, for the broth
- 1/8 tsp ground white pepper, for the broth
- 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴料酒), for the broth

<!-- cup -->

- 2 handfuls soybean sprouts (黄豆芽)

<!-- cup -->

- 3/4 cup peanut oil, for the pour

1. Seat the wok. Press start. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Turn the right dial to 40 minutes. Add the **ginger**, the **scallion whites**, the **unsalted chicken stock**, the **kosher salt for the broth**, the **ground white pepper for the broth**, and the **Shaoxing wine for the broth** together. Stay with it. When it boils, turn the left dial to 250°F. The surface should shiver. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F. Taste. If it is flat, stir in one more pinch of the kosher salt. If you want it, stir in one pinch of MSG (味精).
2. Add the **soybean sprouts**. Cook until they soften but still snap, about 1 minute. Lift them into the heatproof bowl with the wok ladle.
3. Put the **peanut oil for the pour** in a small skillet. Set the skillet fully on a coil turned to medium. Keep the broth at a shiver. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F. When a dry cooking chopstick in the pour oil makes small bubbles and the oil has not smoked, about 4 minutes from when the skillet went on, slide the skillet fully off the coil and turn that coil off. If the oil is smoking, slide the skillet fully off and turn that coil off. Wait until the smoke stops. Leave the skillet off the coil.

## Pour

These amounts were measured above.

**Fish**

- 8.8 oz snakehead fillet (黑鱼), skinless and boneless, sliced 1/8 in thick, marinated

**Prep**

- 3 scallion greens, cut into 2 in lengths
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced thin
- 10 dried erjingtiao (干二荆条), snipped in half, loose seeds shaken out
- 1 tbsp green Sichuan peppercorns (青花椒)

**Broth**

- 3/4 cup peanut oil, for the pour

1. If the fish timer has not rung, wait until it has. Slide in half of the **snakehead fillet**, a few slices at a time. Do not stir. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F. Skim off any foam with the wok ladle. When the slices are white and the edges have curled, about 30 seconds, lift them onto the sprouts with the wok ladle. If a slice is still translucent, leave it 15 seconds more. Cook the remaining half the same way.
2. Ladle the broth over the sprouts and the fish with the wok ladle. The fish should sit in the broth. Take the ginger and the scallion whites with it. Press pause/clear once.
3. Scatter the **scallion greens**, the **garlic**, the **dried erjingtiao**, and the **green Sichuan peppercorns** over the fish until they cover the top.
4. Pour the **peanut oil for the pour** over those aromatics. It should crackle as it lands.
5. Press on/off. Set the wok on its stand. Eat the fish, the sprouts, and the scallion greens. The dried erjingtiao and the green Sichuan peppercorns stay in the bowl for their aroma.

Use small dried red chilies marked 朝天椒. Snip 24 dried facing-heaven chilies (干朝天椒) in half, shake out the loose seeds, and scatter those in place of the dried erjingtiao.

Cover 1 small handful dried wood ear (干黑木耳) with hot water before you start the fish. At 20 minutes, drain it, rinse it, pinch off any hard knobs, and tear it into bites. If you are mid-step, finish it first. Add it with the soybean sprouts and cook until it is soft, about 1 minute.

Rinse 1/2 cup winter bamboo shoots (冬笋). If they are canned or packed in water, slice them thin and add them with the soybean sprouts. If they still have a husk, peel it off, slice them thin, and cover them with water in a heavy saucepan. Set the saucepan fully on a coil turned to high. Stay with it. When the water boils and a piece is no longer hard and bright, pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off. Drain them. Add them with the soybean sprouts.
