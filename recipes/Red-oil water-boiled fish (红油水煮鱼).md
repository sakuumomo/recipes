## Fish

Use unsalted Shaoxing.

- 8.8 oz snakehead fillet (黑鱼), skinless and boneless, sliced 1/8 in thick
- 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴酒), for the fish
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal, for the fish
- 1/4 tsp ground white pepper, for the fish

<!-- cup -->

- 1 egg white

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tbsp sweet potato starch (红薯淀粉)

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tsp peanut oil, for the fish

1. Use the boning knife. If the **snakehead fillet** still has skin, cut the skin off. Slice the fillet on a bias, 1/8 in thick.
2. Rinse the slices under cold water until the water runs clear. Pat them dry.
3. Add the **Shaoxing wine for the fish**, the **kosher salt for the fish**, and the **ground white pepper for the fish** together. Stir until the slices feel sticky.
4. Add the **egg white** and stir until every slice looks slick.
5. Add the **sweet potato starch** and stir until each slice has a thin coat.
6. Drizzle the **peanut oil for the fish** over the top and turn the slices once. Set a timer for 15 minutes. Go on to the prep. When the timer rings, keep going. The fish can wait longer if the broth is not ready yet.

## Prep

Do this while the fish marinates. Use the coarse Pixian paste, and whole dried erjingtiao marked 二荆条.

- 2 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tsp garlic, minced, for the paste
- 1 in ginger, sliced
- 3 scallion whites, cut into 2 in lengths

<!-- cup -->

- 1/2 tsp sugar

<!-- cup -->

- 2 handfuls soybean sprouts (黄豆芽)

<!-- cup -->

- 2 cloves garlic, sliced thin, for the pour
- 10 dried erjingtiao (干二荆条), snipped in half, loose seeds shaken out
- 1 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns (红花椒)
- 3 scallion greens, cut into 2 in lengths

1. Chop the **Pixian doubanjiang**.
2. Mince the **garlic for the paste** with the 8 in cleaver. Slice the **ginger**.
3. Cut the **scallion whites** and the **scallion greens** into 2 in lengths. Keep them in two piles.
4. Rinse the **soybean sprouts** until they look clean.
5. Slice the **garlic for the pour** thin.
6. Snip the **dried erjingtiao** in half and shake out the loose seeds.
7. Put the **garlic for the paste**, the **ginger**, and the **scallion whites** in one cup. Put the **sugar** in a cup. Put the **garlic for the pour**, the **dried erjingtiao**, the **Sichuan peppercorns**, and the **scallion greens** in one cup. Set the cups next to a large heatproof bowl.

## Broth

- 3 tbsp peanut oil, for the paste

<!-- cup -->

- 1 star anise (八角)
- 1 piece cassia bark (桂皮), 2 in

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tsp garlic, minced, for the paste
- 1 in ginger, sliced
- 3 scallion whites, cut into 2 in lengths

<!-- cup -->

- 3 cups unsalted chicken stock
- 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴酒), for the broth
- 1/8 tsp ground white pepper, for the broth
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal, for the broth
- 1/2 tsp sugar

<!-- cup -->

- 3/4 cup peanut oil, for the pour

<!-- cup -->

- 2 handfuls soybean sprouts (黄豆芽)

1. Seat the wok. Press start. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Add the **peanut oil for the paste**. When it shimmers, add the **star anise** and the **cassia bark**. Stir until they smell sweet, about 30 seconds. If the oil smokes, press pause/clear once and stir until the smoke stops. Press start.
2. Add the **Pixian doubanjiang**. Fry, stirring, until the oil is deep red and smells toasty, about 1 minute. If it smells sharp, press pause/clear once and stir until the smell sweetens. Press start.
3. Add the **garlic for the paste**, the **ginger**, and the **scallion whites** together. Stir until fragrant, about 20 seconds.
4. Add the **unsalted chicken stock**, the **Shaoxing wine for the broth**, the **ground white pepper for the broth**, the **kosher salt for the broth**, and the **sugar** together. Stay with it. When it boils, turn the left dial to 250°F. The surface should shiver. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles. Press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F.
5. Put the **peanut oil for the pour** in a small skillet. Set the skillet fully on a coil turned to medium. Simmer the broth until the raw paste smell is gone, about 3 minutes. Lift out the star anise and the cassia bark with the wok ladle. If the pour oil smokes, slide the skillet fully off and turn that coil off. Wait until the smoke stops. Leave the skillet off the coil.
6. Add the **soybean sprouts**. Cook until they soften but still snap, about 1 minute. Lift them with the wok ladle into the heatproof bowl.
7. Keep the broth at a shiver. If the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles. Press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F. If the skillet is already off the coil, leave it off. When it is still on and a dry cooking chopstick in the pour oil makes small bubbles and the oil has not smoked, about 4 minutes from when the skillet went on, slide the skillet fully off the coil and turn that coil off. If the oil is smoking, slide the skillet fully off and turn that coil off. Wait until the smoke stops. Leave the skillet off the coil. Taste. If it is flat, stir in one more pinch of the kosher salt. If you want it, stir in one pinch of MSG (味精).

## Pour

These amounts were measured above.

**Fish**

- 8.8 oz snakehead fillet (黑鱼), skinless and boneless, sliced 1/8 in thick, marinated

**Prep**

- 2 cloves garlic, sliced thin, for the pour
- 10 dried erjingtiao (干二荆条), snipped in half, loose seeds shaken out
- 1 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns (红花椒)
- 3 scallion greens, cut into 2 in lengths

**Broth**

- 3/4 cup peanut oil, for the pour

1. If the fish timer has not rung, wait until it has. Slide half of the **snakehead fillet** into the bottom, a few slices at a time. Do not stir. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F. Skim off any foam with the wok ladle. When the slices are white and the edges have curled, about 30 seconds, lift them with the wok ladle onto the sprouts. If a slice is still translucent, leave it 15 seconds more. Cook the remaining half the same way.
2. Ladle the broth over the sprouts with the wok ladle. The fish should sit in the broth. Press pause/clear once.
3. Scatter the **scallion greens**, the **garlic for the pour**, the **dried erjingtiao**, and the **Sichuan peppercorns** over the fish until they cover the top.
4. Pour the **peanut oil for the pour** over those aromatics. It should crackle as it lands.
5. Brush the wok with hot water. Seat it. Press start. Turn the left dial to 300°F and dry it until the water is gone. Wipe on a little peanut oil, then wipe it back until the metal looks almost dry. Turn the left dial to 450°F. Stop when that film just smokes and the surface looks dry. If it has not smoked, turn the left dial up 10°F. If the smoke thickens, press pause/clear once. Press on/off. The fan may run about a minute. Set the wok on its stand. Eat the fish, the sprouts, and the scallion greens. The chilies and the peppercorns stay in the bowl for their aroma.

---

Cover a handful of dried wood ear (干黑木耳) with hot water before you start the fish. At 20 minutes, drain it, rinse it, pinch off any hard knobs, and tear it into bites. If you are mid-step, finish it first. Add it with the soybean sprouts and cook until it is soft, about 1 minute.

Rinse 1/2 cup winter bamboo shoots (冬笋). If they are canned or packed in water, slice them thin and add them with the soybean sprouts. If they still have a husk, peel it off, slice them thin, and cover them with water in a heavy saucepan. Set the saucepan fully on a coil turned to high. Stay with it. When the water boils and a piece is no longer hard and bright, pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off. Drain them. Add them with the soybean sprouts.

In place of the dried erjingtiao, snip 24 dried facing-heaven chilies (干朝天椒) in half, shake out the loose seeds, and scatter them where the erjingtiao go. Use small dried red chilies marked 朝天椒.
