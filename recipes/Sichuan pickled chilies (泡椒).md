# Sichuan pickled chilies (泡椒)

Makes 1 pint. About 7 days.

The brine heats in a small heavy saucepan, then cools. The chilies pack into a pint jar.

Snip the chilies. Boil the brine and let it cool. Cut the garlic and the ginger while it cools, then pack the jar.

Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.

Lines set close together share a cup.

## Chilies

Use small dried red chilies marked 朝天椒. Use a clean pint jar.

- 1 oz dried facing-heaven chilies (干朝天椒), snipped in half, loose seeds shaken out

1. Snip the **dried facing-heaven chilies** in half and shake out the loose seeds. Put them in the pint jar. Go on to the brine.

## Brine

Use a clear grain liquor marked 白酒.

- 1 1/2 cups water

<!-- cup -->

- 2 1/2 tbsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal
- 1 1/2 tsp sugar

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tbsp baijiu (白酒)

1. Put the **water** in a small heavy saucepan. Set the saucepan on a coil turned to high. Stay with it. Heat until the water boils, about 4 minutes.
2. Pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off. Add the **kosher salt** and the **sugar**. Stir until they dissolve, about 30 seconds.
3. Leave the saucepan off the coil until the brine feels as cool as the counter, about 1 hour. Go on to the aromatics. Come back when the brine is cool.
4. Stir in the **baijiu** until the saucepan smells faintly of liquor, about 10 seconds. Go on to the pack.

## Aromatics

- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 1/2 oz ginger, sliced
- 1/2 tsp Sichuan peppercorns (红花椒)

1. Smash the **garlic** until the cloves split. Slice the **ginger**. The **Sichuan peppercorns** stay whole. Go back to the brine when it feels as cool as the counter.

## Pack

These amounts were measured above.

**Chilies**

- 1 oz dried facing-heaven chilies (干朝天椒), snipped in half, loose seeds shaken out

**Brine**

- 1 1/2 cups water

<!-- cup -->

- 2 1/2 tbsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal
- 1 1/2 tsp sugar

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tbsp baijiu (白酒)

**Aromatics**

- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 1/2 oz ginger, sliced
- 1/2 tsp Sichuan peppercorns (红花椒)

1. Add the **garlic**, the **ginger**, and the **Sichuan peppercorns** to the **dried facing-heaven chilies** in the jar. The aromatics sit on the chilies.
2. Pour the dissolved **water** over them until the chilies are covered. Press the chilies down with a clean spoon. If they float, set a small clean dish on them so they stay under the brine.
3. Set the lid on loosely. The chilies stay under the brine.
4. Leave the jar out of the sun until the brine smells sour and looks a little cloudy and a chili tastes sour, about 7 days. Small bubbles may sit on the chilies during the wait. If a chili tastes only salty, leave the jar 2 days more. Skim a flat white film with a clean spoon when the brine still smells sour. If the brine smells rotten, or the surface grows fuzzy spots or spots that are green, black, or pink, throw the jar out.
5. Screw the lid on and refrigerate. The brine smells sour.
6. Chop the chilies when a page asks for pickled red chilies. Leave the brine in the jar. They are salty, and they keep for a few months.

---

Dried chilies marked erjingtiao (二荆条) use the same weight. They are the milder pepper this pickle is usually made from.

Fresh red chilies are the classic pepper. Use 6 oz in a quart jar in place of the dried chilies. Cut the stems off and wait until the skins look matte, about 2 hours. Double the brine: 3 cups water, 5 tbsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal, 1 tbsp sugar, 2 tbsp baijiu (白酒), 4 cloves garlic, smashed, 1 oz ginger, sliced, and 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns (红花椒). They turn sour in about 10 days.

If there is no baijiu, use 1 tbsp vodka for that line.

Facing-heaven chilies stay hot. Where a page calls for 1 tbsp pickled red chilies, start with 2 tsp.
