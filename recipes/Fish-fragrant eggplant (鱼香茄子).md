# Fish-fragrant eggplant (鱼香茄子)

Serves 2. About 35 minutes.

The eggplant and the sauce cook in the wok, and the wok stays in its ring.

Salt the eggplant. Cut the sauce while it sits. When the 15 minutes are up, fry the eggplant, then cook the pork and the sauce in the same wok.

Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.

Lines with no blank line between them share a cup.

## Ingredients

**Eggplant**

- 12 oz eggplant
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal

- 2 tbsp potato starch

- 1/4 cup peanut oil, for the eggplant

**Sauce**

- 2 oz ground pork

- 1 tbsp pickled red chilies (泡椒), chopped
- 1 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped

- 2 tsp ginger, minced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced

- 3 scallions, whites and greens separated, chopped

- 4 tsp sugar
- 2 tsp light soy sauce (生抽)
- 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴酒)
- 1/4 cup water, for the sauce

- 1 tsp potato starch, for the sauce
- 1 tbsp cold water, for the sauce

- 2 tsp Zhenjiang vinegar (镇江香醋)

---

## Eggplant

Use a long Chinese eggplant.

- 12 oz eggplant
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal

- 2 tbsp potato starch

- 1/4 cup peanut oil, for the eggplant

1. Trim the **eggplant** and cut it into pieces about 2 in long and 3/4 in thick. Toss the pieces with the **kosher salt**. Set a timer for 15 minutes. Go on to the sauce.
2. When the timer rings, squeeze the pieces and pat them dry. Toss them with the **potato starch** until each piece has a thin coat. Shake off the extra starch.
3. Set the wok in the ring on a coil turned to medium-high. Add the **peanut oil for the eggplant**. When it shimmers, about 2 minutes, add a single layer of the **eggplant** in the bottom. Cook until the underside is golden, about 2 minutes. Turn the pieces. Cook until both sides are golden and the center of a piece looks creamy, about 2 minutes more. If the oil smokes, turn the coil off until the smoke stops and the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to medium-high. If the center is still white and spongy, cook 1 minute more. Lift the pieces out.
4. Cook the rest of the **eggplant** the same way, one layer at a time. Lift the pieces out.
5. Turn the coil off. Spoon the oil into a heatproof bowl until about 1 tbsp remains. The wok stays in the ring. If the wok is dry, pour back about 1 tbsp of that oil. Go back to the sauce.

## Sauce

Do this while the eggplant sits. Use ground pork that shows some fat, Sichuan pickled red chilies from the jar marked 泡椒, the coarse Pixian paste, and unsalted Shaoxing.

- 2 oz ground pork

- 1 tbsp pickled red chilies (泡椒), chopped
- 1 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped

- 2 tsp ginger, minced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced

- 3 scallions, whites and greens separated, chopped

- 4 tsp sugar
- 2 tsp light soy sauce (生抽)
- 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴酒)
- 1/4 cup water, for the sauce

- 12 oz eggplant, fried

- 1 tsp potato starch, for the sauce
- 1 tbsp cold water, for the sauce

- 2 tsp Zhenjiang vinegar (镇江香醋)

1. Mince the **ginger** and the **garlic**. Separate the whites and the greens of the **scallions**, and chop both. Chop the **pickled red chilies** and the **Pixian doubanjiang**. Stir the **potato starch for the sauce** and the **cold water for the sauce** together until the liquid is smooth. Go back and finish the eggplant. Come back to the next step when the oil has been poured off.
2. When the coil stops glowing, turn it to medium-high. If the oil smokes, turn the coil off until the smoke stops and the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to medium-high. Add the **ground pork**. Stir, breaking it into small bits, until the pink is gone and the bits look dry and crumbly, about 3 minutes. If the bits are still pink, cook until the pink is gone, about 1 minute more. If they brown hard, turn the coil off and stir until they cool. Wait until the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to medium-high.
3. Add the **pickled red chilies** and the **Pixian doubanjiang** together. Stir until the oil turns red and smells sweet, about 45 seconds. If they smell sharp, turn the coil off and stir until the smell sweetens. Wait until the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to medium-high.
4. Add the **ginger**, the **garlic**, and the whites of the **scallions** together. Stir until they smell sweet, about 20 seconds. If they color, turn the coil off and stir until they cool. Wait until the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to medium-high.
5. Add the **sugar**, the **light soy sauce**, the **Shaoxing wine**, and the **water for the sauce** together. Stir until the sauce bubbles, about 20 seconds.
6. Add the **fried eggplant**. Stir until the pieces are coated and soft, about 1 minute. If a center is still firm, cook 1 minute more. If the sauce sticks, turn the coil off until the sauce loosens and the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to medium-high.
7. Stir the **starch mixture** again. Pour it in. Stir until the sauce looks glossy and clings to the pieces, about 20 seconds.
8. Turn the coil off. Add the **Zhenjiang vinegar** and the greens of the **scallions**. Stir until the vinegar smells sharp and the greens turn bright, about 10 seconds.

---

A globe eggplant works peeled. Weigh 12 oz after peeling. Toss that flesh with 1 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal, and leave it 20 minutes. Rinse, squeeze, and pat it dry, then coat and fry it as written.

For the 2 tsp vinegar, use plain rice vinegar. If the bottle is seasoned sushi vinegar, the sugar line is 2 tsp. If you have balsamic, use 1 1/2 tsp plain rice vinegar and 1/2 tsp balsamic for those 2 tsp.

Cornstarch uses the same spoons as the potato starch, on the eggplant and in the sauce.

If there are no pickled red chilies, chop the Pixian doubanjiang and fry it on its own in that step until the oil turns red and smells sweet, about 45 seconds. With pickled jalapeño, drain and chop 1 tbsp and fry it with the doubanjiang in that same step.
