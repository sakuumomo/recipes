# Fish-fragrant eggplant (鱼香茄子)

Serves 2. About 35 minutes.

Stir-fry in the wok at 375°F. The Mosaic is at 1500 watts.

Salt the eggplant. Cut the sauce while it sits. When the 15 minutes are up, fry the eggplant, then cook the pork and the sauce in the same wok.

Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.

## Eggplant

Use a long Chinese eggplant.

- 2 long Chinese eggplants
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tbsp potato starch

<!-- cup -->

- 1/4 cup peanut oil, for the eggplant

1. With the 8 in cleaver, trim the **long Chinese eggplants** and cut them into pieces about 2 in long and 3/4 in thick. Toss the pieces with the **kosher salt**. Put the **potato starch** in one cup and the **peanut oil for the eggplant** in another. Set a timer for 15 minutes. Go on to the sauce.
2. When the timer rings, squeeze the pieces and pat them dry. If the sauce cups are not finished, let the eggplant sit until they are. Toss the pieces with the **potato starch** until each piece has a thin coat. Shake off the extra starch. Go on to the stir-fry.

1. With the 8 in cleaver, put each group into one cup or pile, prepared the way the line says. The **whites of 3 scallions** and the **greens of 3 scallions** come from the same 3 scallions. Stir the **potato starch for the sauce** and the **cold water for the sauce** together until the liquid is smooth. If the timer has not rung, wait until it does. Go back and finish the eggplant.

## Stir-fry

These amounts were measured above.

**Eggplant**

- 1/4 cup peanut oil, for the eggplant

<!-- cup -->

- 2 long Chinese eggplants, salted and coated

**Sauce**

- 2 oz ground pork

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tbsp pickled red chilies (泡椒), chopped
- 1 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tsp ginger, minced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- whites of 3 scallions, halved lengthwise and chopped

<!-- cup -->

- 4 tsp sugar
- 2 tsp light soy sauce (生抽)
- 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴料酒)
- 1/4 cup water, for the sauce

<!-- cup -->

- 1 tsp potato starch, for the sauce
- 1 tbsp cold water, for the sauce

<!-- cup -->

- 2 tsp Zhenjiang vinegar (镇江香醋)
- greens of 3 scallions, chopped

1. Seat the wok. Press start. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Turn the right dial to 40 minutes. Add the **peanut oil for the eggplant**. When it shimmers, go on. If the oil smokes, press pause/clear once and stir until the smoke stops. Press start.
2. Add a single layer of the **long Chinese eggplants** in the bottom. Cook until the underside is golden, about 2 minutes. Turn the pieces. Cook until both sides are golden and the center of a piece looks creamy, about 2 minutes more. If the center is still white and spongy, cook 1 minute more. If the food stays pale after another minute at this temperature, turn the left dial to 425°F and keep going until both sides are golden and a center looks creamy. Lift the pieces onto a plate.
3. If the dial is not at 375°F, turn the left dial back to 375°F. Add the rest of the **long Chinese eggplants** in the bottom, one layer at a time. Cook that layer until the underside is golden, about 2 minutes. Turn the pieces. Cook until both sides are golden and the center of a piece looks creamy, about 2 minutes more. If the center is still white and spongy, cook 1 minute more. If the oil smokes, press pause/clear once and stir until the smoke stops. Press start. If the food stays pale after another minute at this temperature, turn the left dial to 425°F and keep going. Lift that layer onto the plate. If the dial is not at 375°F, turn the left dial back to 375°F before the next layer.
4. With the wok ladle, spoon the spare oil into a heatproof bowl until about 1 tbsp remains. Leave the wok seated. If the wok is dry, pour back about 1 tbsp of that oil and wait until it shimmers and has stopped smoking.
5. Add the **ground pork**. Stir, breaking it into small bits, until the pink is gone and the bits look dry and crumbly, about 3 minutes. If the bits are still pink, cook until the pink is gone, about 1 minute more. If the bits brown hard, press pause/clear once and stir until they cool. Press start.
6. Turn the left dial to 300°F. When the oil shimmers and has stopped smoking, add the **pickled red chilies** and the **Pixian doubanjiang** together. Stir until the oil turns red and smells sweet, about 45 seconds. If the paste smells sharp, press pause/clear once and stir until the smell sweetens. Press start. If the oil has not turned red, turn the left dial up 10°F and keep frying until it is red.
7. Add the **ginger**, the **garlic**, and the **whites of 3 scallions** together. Stir until they smell sweet, about 20 seconds. If they color, press pause/clear once and stir until they cool. Press start.
8. Turn the left dial to 375°F. Add the **sugar**, the **light soy sauce**, the **Shaoxing wine**, and the **water for the sauce** together. Stir until the sauce bubbles, about 20 seconds.
9. Turn the left dial to 300°F. Add the **long Chinese eggplants** from the plate. Stir until the pieces are coated and soft, about 1 minute. If a center is still firm, cook 1 minute more. If the bottom starts to catch, press pause/clear once and stir until it releases. Press start.
10. Stir the **potato starch for the sauce** and the **cold water for the sauce** again. Pour them in. Stir until the sauce looks glossy and clings to the pieces, about 20 seconds.
11. Press on/off. Add the **Zhenjiang vinegar** and the **greens of 3 scallions**. Stir until the vinegar smells sharp and the greens turn bright, about 10 seconds. Taste. If it is flat, stir in one more pinch of the kosher salt. If you want it, stir in one pinch of MSG (味精).
12. Spoon it onto a plate.

---

A globe eggplant works peeled. Use peeled flesh about the same bulk as the long Chinese eggplants. Toss that flesh with 1 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal, and leave it 20 minutes. Rinse, squeeze, and pat it dry, then coat and fry it as written.

For the 2 tsp vinegar, use plain rice vinegar. If the bottle is seasoned sushi vinegar, the sugar line is 2 tsp. If you have balsamic, use 1 1/2 tsp plain rice vinegar and 1/2 tsp balsamic for those 2 tsp.

Cornstarch uses the same spoons as the potato starch, on the eggplant and in the sauce.

If there are no pickled red chilies, chop the Pixian doubanjiang and fry it on its own in that step until the oil turns red and smells sweet, about 45 seconds. With pickled jalapeño, drain and chop 1 tbsp and fry it with the doubanjiang in that same step.
