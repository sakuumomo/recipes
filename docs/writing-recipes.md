# Writing a recipe

You are writing for one cook, at the stove, in the kitchen described here. The page is what they read while cooking. This document is what you read before you write. Follow these rules. `recipes/Mapo tofu (麻婆豆腐).md` shows the names, the lines, and the shape of a finished page. Where that page and these rules disagree, follow these rules. The same is true of any page still written for the coil and the ring.

A **line** is one ingredient measure. A **part** is one stage the cook finishes. The **gather list** is the `## Ingredients` section. A **heat move** is how this kitchen changes heat. A **coil move** is the exposed-coil stove. A **wok move** is the Mosaic.

## 1. Name the file

Done when the path and the `#` title are fixed.

- Save the file at `recipes/<title>.md`. The directory is flat.
- The title is the headword an English-language cookbook prints: `Mapo tofu`, `Kung Pao chicken`, `Pizza`.
- When that headword renders a name from another language, add a space and the original name in parentheses: `Mapo tofu (麻婆豆腐)`. A spelling that drops diacritics still takes the original: `Pho (phở)`.
- When English already spells the name the same way, the title is the English name alone: `Pizza`.
- The `#` title and the filename are the same words.
- The English name is unique inside `recipes/`. A variant adds the distinguishing words to the English name: `Mapo tofu with beef (麻婆豆腐)`.
- Write the name with spaces and capitals, as it reads on the page.

## 2. Check the dish online

Done when two published recipes have been read and the shared method is chosen.

Search the original name and the English headword. Read at least two published recipes from cooks who know that cuisine. A specialist cookbook, a tested kitchen, or a regional writer outranks an unsourced page.

Keep the cut, the coating, the fat, the defining aromatics, the cook order, and the amounts those sources share. Scale them to this yield. Where the sources disagree, choose the version this kitchen can cook, and put that choice in the steps. A version the cook can skip is a footnote at the end of the page, not a line in the steps. The vessels and the heat moves in this document are what this kitchen can cook.

## 3. Split the dish into parts

Done when every part has a name, a place in the cook order, and a vessel.

A part is a component the cook finishes before it joins the dish, or a stage with its own lines: a blanch, a toasted spice, a slurry, the knife-and-cup prep, the cook in the pan.

Order the parts in the order the cook starts them. A dish cooked as one sequence is one part. That part measures its own lines and cooks them.

When earlier parts measure everything the last part uses, the last part is a combining section. Name it for the action (`Stir-fry`). Every line in it was already measured. It still gets a cooking section.

Rice is its own page, `recipes/Jasmine rice.md`. Do not add a Rice part to another dish.

## 4. Choose the vessel

Done when every part is on the pan this section assigns.

This cook has an electric stove with exposed coils, a NuWave Mosaic induction wok and the 14 in carbon steel wok matched to it, an Instant Pot, and a frying thermometer. The neutral oil is peanut oil. The Mosaic runs at 1500 watts. Press watts until 1500 shows before a cook if a previous session left 600 or 900. The number on the display is the cooker's target. Read a deep pot of oil on the thermometer. Read a thin film of oil by the shimmer.

Only that matched wok sits on the Mosaic. The base beeps and pauses when the wok is off it. Seat the wok again and press start to go on. The wok stays seated while the Mosaic is heating. It goes on its stand when it is off the base.

The heavy saucepan and the small heavy saucepan on the coil are stainless. The Mosaic boils water quickly. A wet cook that is the finished plate can be the wok. Re-season after a pot of water, a broth, a braise, or the steamer. An oil stir-fry does not get that step.

The wok takes a toss, a sear, a shallow fry, a deep fry, a slip, the short sauce that continues in that oil, and a wet cook that is the finished plate. A soy braise and a broth you eat are that wet cook, including when the food is served from a bowl. A poach whose water is thrown out is the saucepan. A ladle of stock, a spoon of vinegar or ketchup at the end, and a few minutes of simmer are part of the oil cook. Fish-fragrant vinegar, a sweet-and-sour glaze, and the ketchup in a short shrimp sauce are that finish. Food can sit up the sides while the bottom reduces. Between a deep fry or a slip and the sauce or toss that follows, the ladle takes the spare oil out. Do not wash the wok in the middle of an oil cook. An empty sear starts in a bare wok.

An intermediate boil goes in the stainless saucepan when the liquid is thrown out, or when this wok still has to take oil in the same cook. A blanch, a poach whose water is discarded, soak water, the twice-cooked simmer, and the water under steaming yolks. Jianghu storm fish is that discarded poach, even though the pour is a skillet. Washing, drying, and re-seasoning would sit between that water and the next oil. A broth or a stew you eat can be the wok. The browning after a discarded blanch, and the braise that is the finished dish, go in the wok. The fond from that browning stays in the wok. The tomato braise stays in the saucepan. A simmer that shares the cook with a fry stays in the saucepan.

A long tomato braise stays in the stainless saucepan for the whole stew. Pour off the blanch water and wipe the saucepan dry before the oil. Once the season wears through, bare iron sits in the tomatoes for the rest of the simmer, and the stew can taste metallic. Re-seasoning after repairs the wok and does not change the food. A spoon of vinegar or ketchup at the end does not do this. When a fry has to happen during a long simmer, that simmer stays in the saucepan and the fry is the wok. The rib beans are that fry.

- **Water thrown out.** The stainless heavy saucepan, whether or not the next oil is this wok. Use the deep one when the food needs the room. For soak water, heat it until it steams, pull the saucepan fully off, and turn the coil off. Leave it until a hand can stay on the side. No finger in the water.
- **A finished soy braise.** The wok, after the blanch, unless a fry has to happen during that simmer. That simmer stays in the saucepan, and the fry is the wok. The rib beans are that fry. Noodles, potatoes, beans, and finish tomatoes wait until the sauce coats a spoon, then go back in.
- **The tomato braise.** The stainless saucepan, blanch through the reduction. Pour off the blanch water and wipe the saucepan dry before the oil. The same wait applies to noodles, potatoes, beans, and finish tomatoes.
- **A brine that heats and then cools.** The small stainless saucepan. The pickles, vinegar or salt. It sits, and it is not the dinner.
- **A small sauce while the wok fries.** The small stainless saucepan.
- **A dry toast.** A small skillet. Peppercorns and sesame. Slide it off when it smells ready, and turn that coil off.
- **A small pour that leaves the pan.** A small skillet. The oil is tipped onto the food or into a bowl: a crackling chili pour, a dumpling-sauce oil. The wide wok holds that oil in a film that will not tip cleanly. Heat it while the broth cooks.
- **A flat lidded crust, or a flat sizzle.** A 10 in heavy skillet. Fried dumplings and pan-fried buns sit flat under a lid, then steam in that same pan. The wok's bowl cannot seat that batch. The sizzling eggplant finishes in this pan, which can heat while the wok fries.
- **The steamer.** The wok, and it is that wok's last job. The basket has to sit in the bowl. Then brush it, dry it, and re-season it.
- **Rice.** Only on `recipes/Jasmine rice.md`. The Instant Pot. Rinse the rice until the runoff is almost clear and drain it well. Use 1 cup of water for each 1 cup of rice. Lock the lid, set the valve to sealing, and pressure cook on high for 3 minutes. When it beeps, stand clear of the valve and turn it to venting. The valve can throw starchy foam. Leave the lid on until serving, then fluff the rice. Do not copy this part into another dish.

### Coil moves

Write these into a step that stayed on the coil. The water drop is for an empty pan. Once oil is in the pan, do not flick water into it to judge the heat. A few tablespoons of oil is too shallow for the thermometer. That oil is ready when it shimmers.

- The pan sits fully on the coil or fully off it.
- Heat the empty 10 in skillet on high until a drop of water skitters and vanishes, about 3 minutes. An empty pan on high is for a sear or a sizzle that gets food as soon as the pan is hot. A small skillet is not heated empty on high. A saucepan of liquid does not get an empty high preheat.
- To lower the heat, pull the pan fully off the coil and turn the coil to the lower setting. If the coil is glowing, wait until it stops. Set the pan fully back on.
- To raise the heat, turn the coil up and leave the pan fully on.
- A coil with no pan on it is off. The glow wait is the exception: a turned-down coil stays on, with the pan off, until the glow is gone. Then the pan goes back, or the coil goes off if the pan is staying off.
- Hold a covered simmer or a shiver on low, with the pan fully on the coil. The surface bubbles gently, or only shivers. Hold an uncovered reduction on medium, with the pan fully on the coil. A long uncovered boil-down keeps the pieces that can sit in the sauce. Noodles, potatoes, beans, and finish tomatoes wait until the sauce coats a spoon, then go back in.
- For soak water, heat it until it steams. Pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off. Leave it until a hand can stay on the side of the saucepan.
- If the pot is on the coil and the center boils harder than the step wants, pull the pan fully off and turn the coil off. When the bubble stops, turn the coil back to the same setting and set the pan fully back on. If that setting boils hard again, do the same again.
- If the pot is on the coil and the surface goes still, turn the coil one setting higher and leave the pan fully on.
- A pot left alone sits fully on a coil that is already at the setting that holds its cue, or it sits fully off and that coil is off. High is a boil you stay and catch.
- When a paste smells sharp, pull the pan fully off and stir until the smell sweetens. Turn the coil to the lower setting. If the coil is glowing, wait until it stops. Set the pan fully back on.
- If the bottom starts to catch, pull the pan fully off and turn the coil off. Stir until it releases. Turn the coil back to the same setting and set the pan fully back on.
- A small skillet slides off the coil the moment it is ready, and that coil goes off.
- A pour onto dried chilies is ready when a dry cooking chopstick makes small bubbles and the oil has not yet smoked. Smoking oil blackens those chilies. A pour that blooms spices in the pan is ready when the oil just starts to smoke. The pan is off the coil, and that coil is off, before the spices go in.
- When a 10 in skillet stays off the coil, turn that coil off. If it comes off between batches, turn that coil off while it is off. Turn the coil back to the setting that holds the fry and set the skillet fully back on when the next batch starts.
- When the cook in that pan is finished, turn its coil off before you plate or eat. An empty pan does not sit on a live coil. A pot of water you are done with does not sit on a live coil.

### Wok moves

Copy the short move into the step. A reason stays here. The left dial moves in 5°F steps from 100°F to 575°F. A nudge that does not name a new temperature is 10°F, two steps. If the cue has not moved, another 10°F. The right dial sets the time. Press start and the Mosaic heats at 375°F for 20 minutes, so the dial comes after start. Do not use Preheat. Preheat takes the empty wok to 575°F, and peanut oil will smoke. Once oil is in the wok, do not flick water into it. A few tablespoons of oil is too shallow for the thermometer. That oil is ready when it shimmers. The cue wins over the clock. Until a cook has timed this wok, a thin layer of peanut oil at 375°F starts from about 30 seconds, and an empty wok at 500°F vanishes a flicked water drop in well under a minute. The step keeps one "about" and the cue. It does not keep this explanation.

Usual temperatures. A step may name any 5°F step from 100°F to 575°F. The dial changes when the job changes. These are the jobs, not a closed list:

- **500°F** is an empty-wok sear. The water drop vanishes, then the oil and the food. The food colors in well under a minute and comes out before the next job.
- **425°F** is only the pale rescue below. Turn it back to the step's temperature before the next batch or the next job.
- **375°F** browns meat, blisters, shallow-fries, brings a pot of liquid to a boil, boils a loose sauce down until it coats a spoon, and cooks the liquid off a pile of wet mushrooms.
- **300°F** is a paste, fermented black beans, or chili flakes fried in oil. It is garlic, ginger, scallions, dried chilies, cumin, and peppercorns. It is wet vegetables, thin beef that should stay tender, a glaze, and a short toss of food that is already cooked. A cold start of that film is 300°F. After a hotter job, the oil shimmers and has stopped smoking before any of this goes in.
- **275°F** holds the boil under a bamboo steamer.
- **250°F** is a covered simmer under the glass lid, a short covered finish, and a broth whose cue is a shiver or a gentle bubble.
- A deep fry and a slip use the temperature the step names. Read it on the thermometer, with the probe in the oil and off the steel. A crust fry is 350°F. A second crisp, read on the thermometer, is 375°F. Coconut shrimp is 325°F. A slip is 275°F, and the food should not brown.

- The first step of a wok part seats the wok, presses start, and then turns the left dial to the temperature. A later temperature change only turns the left dial. A rescue still pauses and then presses start. A part that can run past 20 minutes, including a batched shallow fry, fry oil, a broth, or a covered braise, gets more time on the right dial in the step that starts it.
- For a sear, press start and then turn the left dial to 500°F. Heat the empty wok until a drop of water flicked into the center vanishes. Add the oil and the food then.
- To change temperature, turn the left dial. The wok stays seated.
- After a sear, a brown, or a fry, turn the left dial to 300°F for the paste, the garlic, the ginger, the scallions, or the dried chilies. Add the next oil while the wok is still hot. Hold it until it shimmers and has stopped smoking. An empty hot wok scorches the film left in it.
- Keep the food in the bottom, where the heat is strongest. A sear or a shallow fry goes in batches so the bottom stays hot. Food can sit up the sides while the sauce cooks in the bottom, then fold back down.
- If the oil smokes, press pause/clear once and stir until the smoke stops. Press start.
- If a paste smells sharp, press pause/clear once and stir until the smell sweetens. Press start. If the oil has not turned red, turn the left dial up 10°F and keep frying until it is red.
- If garlic, ginger, or scallions color, press pause/clear once and stir until they cool. Press start.
- If the step is a boil or a reduction and the center boils harder than that, press pause/clear once until the surface settles. Press start. The dial stays where that step set it. If the cue is a shiver, a gentle bubble, or a simmer and the center boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If that surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F.
- If the bottom starts to catch, press pause/clear once and stir until it releases. Press start.
- If the food stays pale after another minute at this temperature, turn the left dial to 425°F and keep going. Turn the left dial back to the step's temperature before the next batch or the next job. A nudge is 10°F, two steps. A step that wants a different heat names that 5°F step.
- If the display reads E7, press pause/clear twice and wait until the fan stops. Seat the wok, set the same temperature, and press start. A long blister can hit this.
- Fry oil and slip oil pool in the bottom. Keep the amount at or under 4 cups. Fry in batches so the pool still covers what the step says it covers. The step that heats the oil stays with the thermometer and turns the left dial to the named temperature after start. If the oil stalls short, turn the left dial up 10°F. If it is still short, another 10°F. If the oil smokes or climbs past the temperature, press pause/clear once and leave the wok seated. When it returns, press start. The next batch says to bring the oil back to that temperature. It does not repeat the stall and the climb. The ladle takes the spare oil out before the sauce or toss. Leave a wok of hot oil seated.
- A covered braise is separate steps. The step that starts it seats the wok, presses start, turns the left dial to 375°F, and gives the simmer enough time on the right dial. Brown at 375°F. Turn to 300°F for the ginger, the garlic, the scallions, and the whole spices, until they smell sweet, then turn back to 375°F and stay until the liquid boils. The next step turns the left dial to 250°F and covers the wok with the glass lid. The surface bubbles gently, or only shivers. If it boils hard, press pause/clear once until the surface settles, turn the left dial down 10°F, and press start. If the surface goes still, turn the left dial up 10°F. If the display reads E7, press pause/clear twice, wait until the fan stops, and start again at the same temperature.
- The reduction is a later step, after the meat is tender. Turn the left dial to 375°F until the sauce coats a spoon. A glaze finishes at 300°F, until it clings and a spoon leaves a clear trail. Noodles that still have to soften in a braise go in at 300°F. A short reduction that cooks the noodles in the sauce from the start stays at 375°F. Potatoes that are already cooked fold back in, and then the heat goes off. Taste after they are back and the heat is off, while you can still stir.
- A short covered finish is its own step: turn the left dial to 250°F, cover with the glass lid, a couple of minutes.
- For a steamer, the water sits about 1 inch below the basket. Press start, then turn the left dial to 375°F. Stay. When the water boils, turn the left dial to 275°F. The water should keep boiling, and the lid should drip. If the boil dies, turn the left dial up 10°F. If the boil reaches the basket, turn the left dial down 10°F. Watch the water so the wok does not boil dry. When the dumplings are out, do the re-season below.
- Lift the wok off only when it holds no hot oil and no boiling water. Set it on its stand. The base pauses while the wok is off it.
- When that cook is finished and the wok does not get a re-season, press on/off before you plate or eat. When the page re-seasons, the food is already out of the wok. Re-season, and press on/off after the oil film has set. The fan may run about a minute. Set the wok on its stand. An empty wok does not sit on a running base. A wok of water you are done with does not sit on a running base.
- Re-season when the wok held a pot of water, a broth, a braise, or the steamer. A spoon of sauce in an oil stir-fry does not get this step. Brush the wok with hot water. Seat it. Press start. Turn the left dial to 300°F and dry it until the water is gone. Wipe on a little peanut oil, then wipe it back until the metal looks almost dry. Turn the left dial to 450°F. Stop when that film just smokes and the surface looks dry. If it has not smoked, turn the left dial up 10°F. If the smoke thickens, press pause/clear once. Do this when the wok is finished, before it sits. Do not do it between a fry and the sauce or toss that follows. No soap on the cooking surface. The base gets a damp cloth, unplugged, and does not go under water.

## 5. Write the lines

## 7. Write the steps

Done when each part has steps a cook can follow while the pan is hot.

- Number the steps. Write them as imperatives.
- Bold the thing the step adds. The bold words match the line's name: the **peanut oil for the beef**, the **drained tofu**. The MSG sentence is the exception. It is not bold, and it has no line.
- A step is one addition or one wait, and it ends on a cue: a look, a smell, or a time. The heat move that brings the pan to that cue stays in the step. Copy that short move from section 4. Leave the reason in section 4. The drop from a hard boil to a shiver or a gentle bubble stays in the step that brought the liquid to a boil, the way the mapo tofu blanch does.
- The first step of a wok part seats the wok, presses start, and then turns the left dial to the temperature. A later temperature change only turns the left dial. A rescue still pauses and then presses start. The step that starts a part longer than 20 minutes sets the time on the right dial.
- A rescue is one sentence in the step that can hit it. The next step does not repeat it. E7 is that rescue on a long blister and on a covered braise.
- The re-season is the last step when the wok held a pot of water, a broth, a braise, or the steamer. The food is already out. The step seats the wok and presses start. Press on/off after the oil film has set. An oil stir-fry does not get it.
- A step does not mention Preheat, the watt button, the temperature range, or why the pan was chosen.
- A taste fix is the one sentence from section 5, on the one step where the sauce, broth, or soup is finished. It is not on every salt line. The MSG sentence is on that step only when the savor is welcome, and nowhere else.
- Finish knife work, rinsing, and measuring in an earlier part when a later step resolves in under a minute. The hot step then names the cups.
- When several cups go in at once, say they go in together.
- A part that sets the cups out tells the cook to put each group into one cup or pile.
- A timer that overlaps the next part tells the cook to go on, and tells them what to do when it rings. The later part that receives that food says what to do if it is still waiting.

Name an ordinary pan, pot, or spoon in the step that uses it. When a common tool produces the result, use it. The knives, the wok spatula, the ladle, the chopsticks, and the wok brush are in section 4. When only an uncommon tool produces the result, put one line under that part's heading and above its lines: `Tool: bamboo steamer.`
