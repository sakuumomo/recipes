# Writing a recipe

You are writing for one cook, at the stove, in the kitchen described here. The page is what they read while cooking. This document is what you read before you write. Follow these rules. `recipes/Mapo tofu (麻婆豆腐).md` shows the names, the lines, and the shape of a finished page. Where that page and these rules disagree, follow these rules.

A **line** is one ingredient measure. A **part** is one stage the cook finishes. The **gather list** is the `## Ingredients` section. A **coil move** is how this stove changes heat.

## 1. Name the file

Done when the path and the `#` title are fixed.

- Save the file at `recipes/<title>.md`. The directory is flat.
- The title is the headword an English-language cookbook prints: `Mapo tofu`, `Kung Pao chicken`, `Pizza`.
- When that headword renders a name from another language, add a space and the original name in parentheses: `Mapo tofu (麻婆豆腐)`. A spelling that drops diacritics still takes the original: `Pho (phở)`.
- When English already spells the name the same way, the title is the English name alone: `Pizza`.
- The `#` title and the filename are the same words.
- The English name is unique inside `recipes/`. A variant adds the distinguishing words to the English name: `Mapo tofu with beef (麻婆豆腐)`.
- Write the name with spaces and capitals, as it reads on the page.

## 2. Check the dish online

Done when two published recipes have been read and the shared method is chosen.

Search the original name and the English headword. Read at least two published recipes from cooks who know that cuisine. A specialist cookbook, a tested kitchen, or a regional writer outranks an unsourced page.

Keep the cut, the coating, the fat, the defining aromatics, the cook order, and the amounts those sources share. Scale them to this yield. Where the sources disagree, choose the version this kitchen can cook, and put that choice in the steps. The vessels and the coil moves in this document are what this kitchen can cook.

## 3. Split the dish into parts

Done when every part has a name, a place in the cook order, and a vessel.

A part is a component the cook finishes before it joins the dish, or a stage with its own lines: a blanch, a toasted spice, a slurry, the knife-and-cup prep, the cook in the pan.

Order the parts in the order the cook starts them. A dish cooked as one sequence is one part. That part measures its own lines and cooks them.

When earlier parts measure everything the last part uses, the last part is a combining section. Name it for the action (`Stir-fry`). Every line in it was already measured. It still gets a cooking section.

Rice is its own page, `recipes/Jasmine rice.md`. Do not add a Rice part to another dish.

## 4. Choose the vessel

Done when every part uses one vessel from this kitchen.

This cook has an electric stove with exposed coils, a round-bottom wok that sits in a ring, an Instant Pot, and a frying thermometer. The neutral oil is peanut oil.

- **Rice.** Only on `recipes/Jasmine rice.md`. The Instant Pot. Rinse the rice until the runoff is almost clear and drain it well. Use 1 cup of water for each 1 cup of rice. Lock the lid, set the valve to sealing, and pressure cook on high for 3 minutes. When it beeps, stand clear of the valve and turn it to venting. The valve can throw starchy foam. Leave the lid on until serving, then fluff the rice. Do not copy this part into another dish.
- **A pot of water.** A heavy saucepan: blanching, boiling, a simmer, and a reduction. A small heavy saucepan is that pot for a brine or a small sauce. A boil is high, and you stay with it. A covered simmer or a shiver is low. An uncovered reduction is medium. A covered braise stays in this pan, so the fond and the liquid stay together.
- **A spoonful of spices, or a small pour of oil.** A small skillet. The pour has to leave the coil, and this skillet can.
- **A flat lidded crust, or a sizzle.** A 10 in heavy skillet. Use it when the food must sit flat under a lid, or the sizzle has to leave the heat. This pan can leave the coil.
- **The round bowl.** The wok, for a toss through the hot bottom, a shallow fry turned in the bottom, a sear, food held up the sides while the bottom reduces, a paste that becomes a sauce, or the water under a bamboo steamer. Use the wok for those. The wok stays in the ring while it is the pan.
- **Deep frying.** A deep heavy saucepan. A slip through a deep pot of oil uses this same pot. The wok does not take this oil. The saucepan has to come fully off the coil. Read the oil on a frying thermometer, in Fahrenheit. The step names the temperature. A slip stays cooler than a crust fry, and the food should not brown.

### Coil moves

Write these into the step that changes the heat. The water drop is for an empty pan. Once oil is in the pan, do not flick water into it to judge the heat. A few tablespoons of oil is too shallow for the thermometer. That oil is ready when it shimmers.

For a skillet or a saucepan:

- The pan sits fully on the coil or fully off it.
- Heat the empty pan on high until a drop of water skitters and vanishes, about 3 minutes for the 10 in skillet. An empty pan on high is for a sear or a sizzle that gets food as soon as the pan is hot. A saucepan of liquid does not get an empty high preheat.
- To lower the heat, pull the pan fully off the coil and turn the coil to the lower setting. If the coil is glowing, wait until it stops. Set the pan fully back on.
- To raise the heat, turn the coil up and leave the pan fully on.
- A coil with no pan on it is off. The glow wait is the exception: a turned-down coil stays on, with the pan off, until the glow is gone. Then the pan goes back, or the coil goes off if the pan is staying off.
- Hold a covered simmer or a shiver on low, with the pan fully on the coil. The surface bubbles gently, or only shivers. Hold an uncovered reduction on medium, with the pan fully on the coil.
- If the pot is on the coil and the center boils harder than the step wants, pull the pan fully off and turn the coil off. When the bubble stops, turn the coil back to the same setting and set the pan fully back on. If that setting boils hard again, do the same again.
- If the pot is on the coil and the surface goes still, turn the coil one setting higher and leave the pan fully on.
- A pot left alone sits fully on a coil that is already at the setting that holds its cue, or it sits fully off and that coil is off. High is a boil you stay and catch. High under a pot of oil is a temperature you stay and read.
- When a paste smells sharp, pull the pan fully off and stir until the smell sweetens. Turn the coil to the lower setting. If the coil is glowing, wait until it stops. Set the pan fully back on.
- A small skillet slides off the coil the moment it is ready, and that coil goes off.
- A pour onto dried chilies is ready when a dry wooden spoon handle makes small bubbles and the oil has not yet smoked. Smoking oil blackens those chilies. A pour that blooms spices in the pan is ready when the oil just starts to smoke. The pan is off the coil, and that coil is off, before the spices go in.
- When a 10 in skillet stays off the coil, turn that coil off.
- Bring deep-fry oil, and oil for a slip, up on high. Stay with the thermometer the whole time that coil is on. Once the oil is at the named temperature, use the lower-heat move to the setting that holds it.
- Between batches, bring the oil back to that temperature on the setting that holds it. When the oil smokes or climbs past that temperature, pull the saucepan fully off and turn the coil off. When the oil returns to that temperature, turn the coil back to the setting that holds it and set the saucepan fully back on.

For the wok:

- Heat the empty wok in the ring on high until a drop of water flicked into the center vanishes, about 2 minutes. The coil glows red quickly. Stop when the drop vanishes. High is for a sear that colors in well under a minute.
- Brown, blister, or boil a sauce down on medium-high. After a high sear, turn to medium-low for a paste or for dried chilies. Turn to medium for wet vegetables, scallions, ginger, and garlic.
- Keep the food in the bottom, where the heat is. The walls stay cooler. Food can sit up the sides while the sauce cooks in the bottom, then fold back down.
- To lower the heat, turn the coil off, wait until it stops glowing, and turn it to the lower setting. The wok stays in the ring.
- After a sear, add the next oil as soon as the coil is off. Hold a paste, black pepper, or dried chilies until that oil shimmers and has stopped smoking. An empty wok scorches the film left in it.
- When the center boils hard, turn the coil off until the surface settles and the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to the setting the step was using.
- When a paste smells sharp, turn the coil off and stir until the smell sweetens. Wait until the coil stops glowing, then turn the coil back to the lower setting.
- When that cook is finished and the coil is off and no longer glowing, the wok can leave the ring.

## 5. Write the lines

Done when the gather list and every part list satisfy this section. Scale the shared amounts from the online check to this yield.

Each line is one measure the cook makes, written `quantity unit name` or `count name`. Two uses of one ingredient are two lines. Each use of water is its own line. Put the role or the prep after a comma: `1 tbsp peanut oil, for the beef`, `2 tsp garlic, minced`, `3 scallions, cut into 3/4 in lengths`.

- Weight is ounces. Volume is teaspoons, tablespoons, and cups. Size is inches. Write fractions as `1/2`, `1/4`, `1/8`, `3/4`. Count garlic by cloves, or measure it by the teaspoon, the tablespoon, or the ounce.
- A spoon measure of kosher salt names the brand after a comma: `1/2 tsp kosher salt, Diamond Crystal`.
- Parentheses hold the original-language name and nothing else, placed after the English name and before any comma: `2 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), chopped`.
- Add that original name when an English search commonly returns a different product. The mapo tofu page is the calibration: soft tofu (嫩豆腐), Sichuan peppercorns (红花椒), Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣酱), fermented black beans (豆豉), Sichuan chili flakes (四川辣椒面). Garlic, ginger, scallions, peanut oil, stock, rice, potato starch, and white pepper stay in English. When a recipe needs a neutral oil, write peanut oil. Do not substitute canola, vegetable oil, or rapeseed oil. A flavored oil stays when the dish needs that flavor, and it keeps its original name: toasted sesame oil (香油).
- When that named product still has kinds a shopper mixes up, the part that first measures it opens with a short sentence naming the kind this page uses. Say it once. Copy one of these sentences, rather than writing a new one for the same product:
  - Use water-packed soft tofu.
  - Use the coarse Pixian paste.
  - Use medium 二荆条 flakes marked 中辣.
  - Use unsalted Shaoxing.
  - Use thin threads with mung bean starch first. A little pea starch is fine, and thick sweet potato noodles go soft and gluey.
  - Use dried sweet potato cords about as thick as a chopstick. Thin mung bean threads fall apart in this stew.
  - Use round white Shanghai-style wheat wrappers from the refrigerator or freezer case.
  - Use thick square wrappers from the refrigerator case. Thin frozen sheets and rice paper will not blister.
  - Use the fatty plate sold as 牛腩, and leave the fat on.
  - Use plain red chili oil.
  - Use flank steak so the slices soften in this time.
  - Use thick Chinese scallions so the pieces stay crisp.
  - Use the thin marbled slices sold for hot pot.
  - Use a waxy potato so the pieces stay whole.
  - Use ripe tomatoes that smell sweet.
  - Use ground pork that shows some fat.
  - Use large peeled shrimp with the tails off.
  - Use cleaned squid tubes.
  - Use the yolks from salted duck eggs.
  - Use unsweetened shredded coconut.
  - Use tomato ketchup.
  - Use plain rice vinegar.
  - Use the thick stem of celtuce, and fresh shiitake.
  - Use a long Chinese eggplant.
  - Use sweet wheat sauce from the jar marked 甜面酱.
  - Use Yibin suimi yacai from the jar marked 碎米芽菜.
  - Use canned pork luncheon meat.
  - Use firm tofu, the block marked 北豆腐, small bok choy, and the thin marbled slices sold for hot pot.
  - Use Sichuan pickled red chilies from the jar marked 泡椒.
  - Use toasted Chinese sesame paste.
  - Use small dried red chilies marked 朝天椒.
  - Use a clear grain liquor marked 白酒.
  - Flat green beans and common green beans both turn silky in this stew.
  - Flat green beans and common green beans both blister in this fry.
- The gather list contains each line once, under the bold label of the part that first measures it. Labels follow cook order. Inside a part, lines follow use order.
- A blank line inside a part splits one cup from the next. Lines with no blank line between them share a cup. A cup is what one step adds together, or adds one after another before that step's cue, when those things fit in one container. Oil that heats alone is its own cup. A bulky piece is its own cup. A small seasoning added with that piece can sit in the same cup. The whites and the greens of a scallion stay in different cups when the greens go in later.
- The gather list, the part list, and the combining list use the same breaks.
- Every part that measures a line repeats it with the same words.
- A later part may append only the state the ingredient is in now: `, drained`, `, toasted and crushed`.
- The combining section repeats the lines it uses. Those repeats are the same measures, grouped under the same bold labels, in the same order, with the same blank lines.

When you change a line, change every copy of it.

## 6. Assemble the page

Done when the page matches this shape. Three sentences in the template are exact: `Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.` and `Lines with no blank line between them share a cup.` and `These amounts were measured above.` The yield line follows the pattern below. The numbers, vessels, and part names in the template are examples.

```markdown
# English name (原文)

Serves 2. About 30 minutes.

<One line naming the vessel and program for each part.>

<One line for the order of work when a pot waits or a timer interrupts.>

Measure each ingredient once. A later list is that same amount again.

Lines with no blank line between them share a cup.

## Ingredients

**First part**

- 1 cup ingredient
- 1 tsp ingredient

- 2 tbsp ingredient

**Second part**

- 4 oz ingredient

---

## First part

- 1 cup ingredient
- 1 tsp ingredient

- 2 tbsp ingredient

1. Do the thing with the **ingredient** until the cue. Go on to the next part.

## Second part

Do this before the pan is hot.

- 4 oz ingredient

1. Do the thing until the cue.

## Stir-fry

These amounts were measured above.

**First part**

- 1 cup ingredient
- 1 tsp ingredient

- 2 tbsp ingredient

**Second part**

- 4 oz ingredient, drained

1. Add the **ingredient** and cook until the cue.
```

- The yield line is `Serves N. About M minutes.`, `Makes N. About M minutes.`, or `Makes N. About D days.` The time runs until the cook eats or the jar is ready, including waits that overlap other parts.
- The setup line names the Instant Pot program and the pan for each part that has one.
- The order line is there when the cook starts a part and leaves it. It names the interrupt: when the timer rings, what they do, then that they keep going.
- The measure sentence and the cup sentence are on every page.
- The gather list is one `## Ingredients` section. Its bold labels are the parts, so the outline stays the cooking sections. Inside a label, a blank line marks a new cup.
- A `---` sits between the gather list and the first cooking section.
- Cooking sections are `##` headings in cook order.
- A part may open with one or two short sentences under the heading. The product-kind sentence is the one in section 5, under the part that first measures it. Prep that must finish before heat says so. The combining section says `These amounts were measured above.`
- Ingredient bullets sit under the heading or the bold label. The numbered steps follow them.
- Keep each numbered list on consecutive lines, so the numbers stay one series.
- Filename, heading, and parenthesis rules stay in this document.

## 7. Write the steps

Done when each part has steps a cook can follow while the pan is hot.

- Number the steps. Write them as imperatives.
- Bold the thing the step adds. The bold words match the line's name: the **peanut oil for the beef**, the **drained tofu**.
- A step is one addition, one wait, or one coil move, and it ends on a cue: a look, a smell, or a time.
- A coil move that has its own cue is its own step. Write it with the coil moves in this document.
- A rescue stays in the step that can hit it. Leave out a following sentence that only repeats that rescue.
- Finish knife work, rinsing, and measuring in an earlier part when a later step resolves in under a minute. The hot step then names the cups.
- When several cups go in at once, say they go in together.
- A part that sets the cups out tells the cook to put each group into one cup or pile.
- A timer that overlaps the next part tells the cook to go on, and tells them what to do when it rings. The later part that receives that food says what to do if it is still waiting.

Name an ordinary pan, pot, or spoon in the step that uses it. When a common tool produces the result, use it. When only an uncommon tool produces the result, put one line under that part's heading and above its lines: `Tool: bamboo steamer.`

## 8. Check, then save

Done when every item below holds and the file is saved at `recipes/<title>.md`.

- The amounts and the method were checked against at least two published recipes. Where those recipes disagree, the page uses the version this kitchen can cook.
- The path is `recipes/<title>.md`, the `#` title matches, and the English name is unique.
- An original name sits in parentheses when the English title renders another language.
- The header has the yield line, the setup line, the order line when something waits, the measure sentence, and the cup sentence.
- The gather list is `## Ingredients` with bold labels. Each line appears there once. Inside a label, a blank line marks a new cup, and the same breaks appear in the part list and the combining list.
- A combining section is absent from the gather list and present among the cooking sections, with its amounts grouped by the part that measured them. A one-part dish has no combining section.
- A `---` sits between the gather list and the first cooking section. Cooking sections are `##` headings in cook order.
- Every line is one measure, with a quantity and a unit or a count. Units are ounces, teaspoons, tablespoons, cups, and inches, written as fractions. Count garlic by cloves, or measure it by the teaspoon, the tablespoon, or the ounce.
- Labels in the gather list follow cook order. Inside a part, lines follow use order. Lines with no blank line between them share a cup: what one step adds together, or adds one after another before that step's cue, when those things fit in one container. Oil that heats alone is its own cup. A bulky piece is its own cup, and a small seasoning added with it can share that cup.
- Copies of a line match. A later copy adds only the current state.
- An ingredient that an English search commonly misidentifies carries its original name. The mapo tofu page is the calibration.
- When a named product still has kinds a shopper mixes up, the part that first measures it names the kind in a short sentence. A choice already written on another page is copied.
- A spoon measure of kosher salt names Diamond Crystal. Parentheses hold only an original-language name.
- The neutral oil is peanut oil. A flavored oil is named only when the dish needs that flavor.
- Rice stays on `recipes/Jasmine rice.md`. Another dish has no Rice part. Every part uses the vessel this kitchen assigns it.
- Heat changes use the coil moves. A saucepan sits fully on the coil or fully off it. A coil with no pan on it is off. A wok stays in the ring.
- Deep-fry oil, and oil for a slip, is a Fahrenheit temperature on a thermometer. The step names the temperature, the cook stays with the thermometer while that coil is on, and the oil returns to the temperature between batches. A shallow pan of oil does not.
- Steps are a tight numbered list. The added thing is bold. Each step ends on a cue.
- Work that a short step depends on is finished in an earlier part.
- An uncommon tool has one `Tool:` line. Every other tool is named in the step.
